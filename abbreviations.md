# DW Gold Trading Knowledge Base

DW Gold Trading is an education-only WhatsApp bot for beginner gold trading questions. The bot must not give trading signals, financial advice, investment advice, buy or sell recommendations, or market predictions.

## Stop Loss
A stop loss, also called SL, is a safety line on a trade. It tells the platform to close the trade if price moves against you by a set amount. In simple terms, it is there to stop one bad trade becoming a massive loss. A beginner should always understand the stop loss before placing a live trade.

## Support
Support is a price area where gold has previously struggled to fall lower. Think of it like a floor. Price may bounce from support, but it is never guaranteed. Support is used for education and chart understanding only, not as a buy signal.

## Resistance
Resistance is a price area where gold has previously struggled to rise higher. Think of it like a ceiling. Price may reject from resistance, but it is never guaranteed. Resistance is used for education and chart understanding only, not as a sell signal.

## Risk Management
Risk management means protecting your money before thinking about profit. It includes using sensible lot size, using a stop loss, avoiding overtrading, and not risking money you cannot afford to lose. In simple terms: protect the account first, profit comes second.

## Take Profit
Take profit, also called TP, is the price level where a trade closes in profit automatically. It is like setting a target before the trade starts. It helps beginners avoid getting greedy or emotional.

## Pips
On gold, people often use the word pips loosely. Gold usually moves in points and price decimals. For example, if XAUUSD moves from 2300.00 to 2301.00, that is a 1 dollar move. Different brokers may display points differently, so always check your MT5 platform carefully.

## Points
Points are the small movements shown on the trading platform. On gold, points can depend on how the broker displays XAUUSD. Beginners should focus on the actual price movement and how much money that movement equals for their lot size.

## Spread
Spread is the difference between the buy price and the sell price. It is basically the broker's price gap. If the spread is high, the trade starts further in loss. Beginners should always check the spread before placing any trade.

## Lot Size
Lot size is the size of the trade. Bigger lot size means bigger profit if right, but also bigger loss if wrong. For beginners, small lot sizes are safer while learning. Lot size should match the account size and risk plan.

## Leverage
Leverage lets a trader control a bigger trade with a smaller amount of money. It can increase gains, but it can also increase losses very quickly. Beginners must treat leverage with respect because it can damage an account fast.

## Margin
Margin is the amount of money held by the broker to keep a trade open. It is not a fee, but it is locked while the trade is running. If there is not enough margin, trades may not open or may be closed by the platform.

## Free Margin
Free margin is the money available to open new trades or handle movement against existing trades. If free margin gets too low, the account becomes risky. Beginners should keep plenty of free margin and avoid maxing out the account.

## Equity
Equity is the live value of the account including open trades. If open trades are in profit, equity is higher. If open trades are in loss, equity is lower. Equity moves while trades are open.

## Balance
Balance is the account value after closed trades only. It does not include floating profit or floating loss from trades that are still open. Balance changes when a trade is closed, money is deposited, or money is withdrawn.

## Drawdown
Drawdown is how much the account drops from a higher value. For example, if an account goes from £1,000 to £900, that is a £100 drawdown. Big drawdown is dangerous because it means the account is under pressure.

## Candles
Candles show price movement on a chart for a chosen time period. On an M5 chart, each candle shows 5 minutes of price movement. Candles help traders understand open, high, low, and close prices.

## Wicks
Wicks are the thin lines above or below a candle body. They show where price reached but did not close. A long wick can show rejection, but it is not a guaranteed signal.

## Bullish Candles
A bullish candle means price closed higher than it opened. In simple terms, buyers pushed price up during that candle. A bullish candle alone is not a reason to buy.

## Bearish Candles
A bearish candle means price closed lower than it opened. In simple terms, sellers pushed price down during that candle. A bearish candle alone is not a reason to sell.

## Doji Candles
A doji candle means price opened and closed close to the same level. It can show indecision between buyers and sellers. A doji needs context and should not be treated as a signal by itself.

## Trend
A trend is the general direction price is moving. An uptrend has higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend has lower highs and lower lows. Trends can change at any time.

## Consolidation
Consolidation is when price moves sideways in a range instead of strongly up or down. It can show the market is waiting for direction. Beginners should be careful because sideways markets can be choppy.

## FVG
FVG stands for Fair Value Gap. It is a gap or imbalance on the chart where price moved quickly. Some traders watch these areas because price may return to them, but it is not guaranteed and it is not a signal by itself.

## Liquidity
Liquidity means areas where orders may be sitting, often around highs, lows, or obvious stop-loss areas. Price can move toward liquidity before changing direction. This is an education concept, not a prediction tool.

## Break Even
Break even, also called BE, means moving the stop loss to the entry price so the trade should not lose money if price returns. It can protect a trade, but moving to break even too early can also close trades before they develop.

## Demo Accounts
A demo account uses practice money, not real money. It is useful for learning MT5, testing lot size, understanding SL and TP, and building confidence before risking real money.

## MT5
MT5 stands for MetaTrader 5. It is a trading platform used to view charts, place trades, set stop losses, set take profits, and manage trading accounts. Beginners should practise on demo before using real money.

## Vantage
Vantage is a broker/platform provider that some traders use with MT5. DW Gold Trading may help people understand setup steps, but users must read the broker terms, risk warnings, deposit rules, and withdrawal rules themselves.

## MarketVox
MarketVox is treated as a platform or service name in the DW Gold Trading setup process. The bot can explain basic setup and education steps only. Users should check official MarketVox information for account, payment, and service details.

## Deposits
A deposit means adding money to a broker or platform account. Before depositing, users should understand the platform, the risks, the terms, and whether they can afford the risk. Never deposit money you cannot afford to lose.

## Withdrawals
A withdrawal means taking money out of a broker or platform account. Withdrawal times, fees, checks, and methods can vary by provider. Users should check the official platform instructions and make sure their identity verification is complete.

## Gold Trading Terminology
Gold trading terminology includes words like XAUUSD, spread, lot size, margin, equity, stop loss, take profit, support, resistance, FVG, liquidity, bullish, bearish, and drawdown. Beginners should learn these words before placing live trades.
